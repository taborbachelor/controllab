ControlLab — Live

A simulated line, running in real time

This is a small simulated factory line that moves bulk material (think grain or gravel) from a storage bin, through a gate and a feeder, up a conveyor and into a hopper. A control program runs it, the way a PLC runs a real plant: you operate it, break it on purpose, and watch it protect itself and recover.

What am I looking at?
  • What's happening (the card below) says what the line is doing right now, why, and what to do next.
  • The picture is the line. Dark shapes are running or open, hollow ones are stopped or closed, dashed amber means "told to move but not confirmed yet", red means tripped. Material shows as brown. Hover anything for what it is.
  • Operator buttons are what a plant operator has: Start, Stop, Acknowledge (confirm you've seen an alarm), and Reset (clear a trip once its cause is fixed).
  • Guided walkthroughs run short stories step by step: a normal start, an emergency stop, a failure and its recovery.
  • Engineer tools break things on purpose (motor faults, a jammed feeder, a failed sensor) and set levels, to test how the control program reacts. That's the point of ControlLab: proving control logic works, faults included, before any real equipment exists.
  • Alarms and the event log record what the controller noticed and did, the way a real control room does.

    Instrument faults

    Stuck freezes the reading (a seized float, a frozen transmitter): nothing in the signal says it's wrong. Failed is a lost signal: WT-105 reads 0 with its channel diagnostic set; a switch reads 0, which the fail-safe level switches report as high. Try LSHH-105 stuck, then set the hopper to 97 %: WT-105 still trips the line (1oo2), and the cross-check names the switch.

    These act on the simulated equipment, never on the control program: the program has to notice and react on its own. A highlighted button is a fault currently injected; click it again to clear it. A motor trip also needs its device reset (Field resets) before the line's Reset will take, the same two-step recovery a real line has.

    Alarms

    An alarm stays listed (latched) until its cause is gone and someone presses Acknowledge. FIRST OUT marks the one that started it.
